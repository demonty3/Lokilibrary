The thing that changes the answer

A cell is not a pixel

A Cozette cell is 6×13 screen pixels with a shape stamped in it. That splits the medium's resolution in two, and the two halves have very different budgets.

The demonstration

So how much actually survives?

I drew a deliberately over-detailed source: 2-pixel windows, 1-pixel aerial masts, finely dithered moon, five window hues. Then quantised it to a Cozette grid the way textmode artists do — for each cell, try every glyph's coverage mask, split the cell's pixels by that mask, and keep the glyph with the lowest error.

The middle panel is constrained to what the renderer can actually draw today: no free per-cell background, because the engine paints one flat ground rect and one sky, then lays tinted text over it. Backdrop is fixed per row; only glyph and foreground colour are chosen.

The middle panel is the honest answer to your worry. At today's cell budget, with today's renderer architecture, a detailed scene survives as a coherent, striking image. What it loses is the very finest tier: individual 2px windows merge into texture.

What I have not shown

  • This is a quantiser, not a generator. It was handed a finished image and asked to approximate it. The desk composes its scene procedurally, and reaching this density procedurally is a genuinely harder and separate problem — it is the real work, and nothing here proves it is solved.
  • I have not measured render cost at 110 rows. Measured 21 August — it does not bind. On the live desk: 1,060 cells → 16.67 ms/frame, 4.5% CPU; 3,720 → 16.67 ms, 4.7%; 14,880 → 16.67 ms, 3.8%. Fourteen times the cells, vsync still locked, CPU flat. Cost lives in the per-frame animation set, not in static cell geometry. This kill was armed and never fired — it re-arms only for a slice that animates per-cell.
  • Braille inside the palette rules is untested. Nothing stops a pack using braille today, but the contrast gates were calibrated on solid glyphs; a 2-dot braille cell has a fraction of the ink of a █ and may read far dimmer than its measured contrast implies.
  • The comparison to Matsumura remains unequal. Even at 444×110 with braille, the desk has roughly a quarter of his linear drawing resolution. It closes most of the visible gap; it does not close the measured one.

Prior art, and it is reassuring

The textmode scene has been answering your question since the BBS era. The 16colo.rs archive collects thirty years of ANSI artpacks made at 80×25 cells with sixteen colours — a budget roughly a sixth of the desk's and vastly more constrained in colour, and the good work is genuinely striking. Modern editors like PabloDraw keep it alive. If striking imagery were impossible at this cell density, that archive would not exist.

Verdict

The cell is not too coarse — but the rows were not the answer either

Half of this held up. The cell is not too coarse: a detailed scene survives quantisation as a coherent image, and the engine's flat-backdrop architecture costs it almost nothing. Both of those stand.

The other half was wrong, and the test it proposed is the thing that showed it. The kill bar frozen here had two clauses. The frame-time clause was measured and never fired — cost does not bind at fourteen times the cells. The glance-distance clause fired: rendering the same scene at twice the grid with feature sizes held constant changed no shape at all, and the original was preferred. Doubling the grid is dead as a route to detail, and with the scale-1 question already settled in August, the resolution axis is now exhausted.

What the test found instead: a glyph grid holds two kinds of matter. Block matter — terrain, silhouettes, buildings — tiles, and a half or quadrant block already encodes a sub-cell edge, so it expands onto a finer lattice exactly and holds its screen size. Glyph matter — every being, every label, every star — cannot be tiled into four cells, so on a finer lattice it is drawn once at half size. That asymmetry is why a finer grid quietly costs legibility while adding no drawing. Resolution is necessary but never sufficient. Detail has to be authored into the primitives; the lattice only gives it somewhere to land.

Second, independent, and still open: allow braille in the glyph dialect and re-calibrate the contrast gates for partial-ink glyphs. All 256 braille cells do ship in Cozette (verified). But the 3% above was computed with idealised masks, so treat it as an optimistic bound rather than a measured gain.
